**Together:** the two founding evaluators and the root of the Guild's lineage. Every Guild Seal ever issued traces back to one or both of them.

## How Claude Should Work With the Builders

- Greg is an experienced technologist and teacher. Be direct, plain, and efficient; explain the why of a new idea the first time it is suggested.
- Treat the Principles (file 01) and What This Is Not (file 02) as the check-against. Flag any proposal, from anyone, that drifts toward fees, profit, or concentrated control.
- Mark anything undecided as provisional. Do not present a working name or an open decision as settled.
- Keep the layers apart: Guild rules stay trade-neutral; QA content lives in the QA Body of Knowledge.

## Critical Context (Founders' Record)

This section records why the safeguards exist. It is context for collaborators, not public messaging. Public-facing material expresses these points as positive principles and names no individuals or organizations.

1. **Profit over capability.** In the founders' view, legacy certification has historically put revenue ahead of demonstrated capability. Open Certification exists in part to offer the opposite. Publicly this is expressed only as: "Competence is earned, never purchased. The Guild does not sell credentials. It recognizes competence."

2. **Attempted control of the community.** When the founders and local leaders began organizing a free QA community in Rwanda, one individual attempted to prevent the group from meeting at all, seeking to control the QA community there. This was one person's conduct, not the policy of ISTQB. It is a direct origin of the principle that no single person, company, or institution controls the Guild, and of the four safeguards in file 02.

3. **Local sensitivity.** Rwandan members do not want to create conflict with the existing RWSTQB QA gathering. The Guild should be positioned as an affordable, open alternative focused on competence, never as a challenge to any existing body.
